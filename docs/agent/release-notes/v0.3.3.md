## What's new in Armory 0.3.3

Install 0.3.3 on every computer. Installing it over 0.3.2 keeps your Armory folder, your files and your sign-in. This update comes from every note and report you sent from Armory. Thank you.

**Your saved work is never put back to an older version.** If you clicked Check in while the part was still open in SolidWorks, Armory could let the file go without your last saves and then put the team's older version back on your computer. Your work was kept on the server as your own copy, but your file looked like it had lost your changes. Now Armory never checks in a file that SolidWorks still has open: it says "Plate.SLDPRT is open in SolidWorks. Save it there and close it; Armory checks it in as soon as it's closed," and it checks it in the moment you close it. If this happened to you, open the file's page in Armory and press **Put back on this computer** on your copy.

**SolidWorks years are checked for real.** Earlier versions never actually checked which SolidWorks year saved a file, so a part saved in SolidWorks 2026 could reach the team and stop opening on the school's SolidWorks 2025 computers. Armory now reads the year from the file itself. A file saved in a newer year than your project uses stays on your computer only, with a plain message, and is never sent to the team. Files sent before this version are checked too: Armory tells you which ones were saved in 2026 and what someone with SolidWorks 2026 needs to do to fix them.

**Armory works with SolidWorks directly.** While SolidWorks is open, Armory sees which files you open and save. You don't set anything up, and nothing is added inside SolidWorks.
- **Open a file you haven't checked out,** and Windows shows a notification: "Check out Plate.SLDPRT to edit it?" Click **Check out and reopen**, and the file becomes editable right where it is. Armory never checks a file out just because you opened it.
- **On a computer with SolidWorks 2026 (Service Pack 3 or newer),** when you save a team file, Armory saves it in the team's year (2025), at the same place with the same name, so every assembly still finds it. Before you save, it tells you what SolidWorks 2025 can't keep (for example colors or explode steps). If something in the file can't go back to 2025, it stays on your computer only and Armory tells you what to change.

**Right-click in File Explorer.** Inside the Armory folder, right-click a file or folder and choose **IDEA Armory**: Check out, Check out and open, Check in, Undo check out, or Show in Armory (mentors and CAD leads also get Force check in). On Windows 11 it's under **Show more options**. Armory answers with its usual one sentence.

**Status badges on file icons (optional).** Files in the Armory folder can show a small badge: a pencil for files you have checked out, a padlock for files someone else has, a red "!" for files that need you, and a check for files that are up to date. Turning badges on needs an administrator once per computer: choose "Also show Armory's status on file icons" at the end of the installer, or run "Show Armory status on file icons" from the flash drive, or press **Turn on** in Settings. Settings tells you if Windows isn't showing them because other apps (like OneDrive) use up its badge spots.

**Taking turns on a lab computer.** In Settings, turn on **This computer is shared by several students**. Then, when Armory's window opens, it asks who you are, like Chrome does: click your name, type your 4-digit PIN, and you're in, with no browser sign-in after the first time. **Add a student** signs a new student in once. If the last student still has files checked out, their name says so, and you can wait for them or keep working in a folder of your own. Nothing of anyone's is ever deleted when you switch or remove a student.

**Big downloads keep going.** Downloading hundreds of files no longer stops and starts with "looking for updates" between batches. The progress line keeps counting, and Armory says it's finished only once everything is here.

**Every click answers at once.** Check out, check in, Open and the other buttons no longer wait behind a sync of the whole folder. The window shows what's happening the moment you click, and a long check out or check in shows lines of what Armory is doing as it goes.

**Hover any button to see what it does.** Every button now explains itself after about a second.

**Send feedback, like the website's.** Pick Bug, Idea, Praise or Other, say what you tried, and add a picture of the Armory window if it helps (you see exactly what is sent, and other people's addresses are hidden). **Your feedback** in Settings lists what you've sent and whether the IDEA team has read it.

**Files with a name already in the project.** A copy of a file whose name another file in the project already has can't be added (every name is used once per project). Armory now says so once, tells you what to do (rename it in SolidWorks, or use the team's copy), and stops trying again and again, so your computer no longer shows "Uploading 0 of 142 files" forever.

**Mentors and CAD leads can tidy up.** Renaming or deleting a folder, or renaming a file, that someone else has checked out now offers to force check in their files first; anything they hadn't checked in is kept as their own copy.

**Force check in many files is one quick step,** and says how many were checked in and how many weren't checked out any more.
